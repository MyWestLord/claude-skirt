Fly Brain

How it works

Her heading lives in a ring of 16 E-PG neurons, like the compass in a fruit fly's central complex. A bump of activity sits on the ring. When the choreography asks for a turn, P-EN neurons push the bump around the ring, and her body follows the bump. Δ7 neurons inhibit the whole ring so only one bump survives.

A landmark input pins the bump to where she should face, the way a fly uses the sun. Turn it off and small errors pile up while she spins.

Her steps come from a half-center oscillator: two neurons that inhibit each other and tire out in turn. Each beat of the music reaches it through the antennae, which is how flies hear.

Built without Blender

The character is generated from math at load time: lathes, sweeps and superellipsoids. Arms and legs use two-bone IK. The renderer is raw WebGL2, and the music is synthesized live with Web Audio.
